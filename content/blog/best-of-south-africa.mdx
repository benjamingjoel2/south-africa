South Africa is the size of France, Germany and the UK combined. It has two oceans, seven climate zones and more variety than most travellers expect. That makes a first trip thrilling to plan, and easy to over-stuff. Here's how we think about it.

## Start at the Cape

There's a reason almost every great South Africa itinerary begins in **Cape Town**. Table Mountain hangs over the city, the Atlantic beaches are a ten-minute drive from the centre, and the food scene punches far above its weight. Give it **four or five nights**. That's enough to wait out a windy day for the cableway and still drive the Cape Peninsula to Cape Point and the penguins at Boulders Beach.

Then go an hour inland to the **Cape Winelands**. Stellenbosch and Franschhoek are surrounded by mountains and vineyards, and staying two nights (rather than day-tripping) turns tastings into long lunches and lazy sunsets.

## Add the safari

For most first-timers, the safari is the emotional heart of the trip. The simplest combination is a short flight from Cape Town to the **Greater Kruger** area, where private reserves like the Sabi Sand and Timbavati offer:

- Off-road tracking with a ranger and tracker
- A limit on the number of vehicles at each sighting
- All-inclusive lodges, with two game drives a day

Three nights is the minimum; four is the sweet spot. Not sure which kind of safari suits you? Read our guide to [Kruger vs private reserves](/blog/kruger-vs-private-reserves).

## If you have more time

With **two weeks or more**, consider adding one of these:

- **The Garden Route:** a self-drive (or chauffeured) road trip along the south coast, with forests, lagoons and Plettenberg Bay's beaches. Finish with a malaria-free safari in the Eastern Cape.
- **Hermanus:** between June and November, southern right whales come into Walker Bay to calve, often within metres of the cliffs.
- **The Drakensberg & KwaZulu-Natal:** mountain hikes, San rock art, Zulu history and warm Indian Ocean beaches.
- **Johannesburg & the Panorama Route:** the Apartheid Museum and Soweto, then the escarpment viewpoints of Blyde River Canyon on your way to Kruger.

## A classic first-timer's shape

| Nights | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 4–5 | Cape Town | Mountain, coast, food, history |
| 2 | Winelands | Slow down, eat and drink well |
| 3–4 | Greater Kruger | The Big Five, with expert guides |
| Optional 3–6 | Garden Route, Hermanus or KZN | Depth and variety |

> Our golden rule: fewer places, longer stays. Two nights per stop is the minimum we recommend.
