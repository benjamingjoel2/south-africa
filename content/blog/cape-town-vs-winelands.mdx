Cape Town and the Winelands are only about an hour apart, so many travellers ask whether they need to move hotels at all. Our short answer: **yes, if you can**. They're very different experiences. Here's why.

## Staying in Cape Town

**Choose the city if you want:** beaches, restaurants within walking distance, easy access to Table Mountain, Robben Island and the Cape Peninsula.

Our favourite areas:

- **City Bowl & Tamboerskloof:** leafy streets below Lion's Head, close to Kloof Street's cafés and bars.
- **Camps Bay & Clifton:** beachfront glamour and sunsets, a short drive from town.
- **V&A Waterfront:** convenient and secure, with harbour views and plenty of dining.
- **Constantia:** a green, quieter alternative with its own historic wine estates.

## Staying in the Winelands

**Choose the Winelands if you want:** slower days, long lunches, mountain views and wine tasting without watching the clock.

- **Franschhoek:** a compact village with one main street of restaurants, ringed by mountains. Romantic and walkable.
- **Stellenbosch:** a historic university town with more estates and a livelier feel.
- **The Helderberg & Somerset West:** close to the airport, and perfect for a first or final night.

## Why not just day-trip?

You can, and a private driver makes it easy. But staying overnight means:

1. No rushing back for dinner in the city
2. Sunset tastings and dinner at the estate
3. A morning walk or ride through the vineyards before the day-trippers arrive

## How we usually split it

> **4 nights Cape Town + 2 nights Winelands** is our favourite combination for a first visit.

If you're heading on to a safari, finish in the Winelands. It's close to the airport and a lovely way to slow down before the bush.
